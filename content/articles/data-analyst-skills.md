The core data analyst skills are Excel, SQL, one BI tool such as Power BI or Tableau, basic statistics and, increasingly, Python with pandas. Alongside these, employers hire for business sense, clear communication and the ability to tell a story with data. For a first job you do not need to master everything; you need working fluency in Excel and SQL, one dashboard tool, and visible proof that you can turn a messy dataset into a useful answer.

## The short list

These are the skills that appear again and again in entry-level data analyst job descriptions on Naukri, LinkedIn and Instahyre:

- Excel: cleaning, lookups, conditional totals, pivot tables.
- SQL: filtering, aggregating, joining tables, basic window functions.
- A BI tool: Power BI or Tableau, enough to build an interactive dashboard.
- Statistics: averages, spread, distributions, correlation, basic hypothesis testing.
- Python: pandas for cleaning and analysis, matplotlib or seaborn for charts.
- Business sense: knowing which numbers matter to the business and why.
- Communication and storytelling: explaining findings to people who do not read SQL.

The order is roughly the order of importance for a fresher role in India. Many IT services companies, GCCs and BFSI firms screen first on SQL and Excel, while startups and product companies are more likely to also test Python.

## Technical data analyst skills

### Excel

Excel is still where much business reporting starts and ends, especially in finance, operations and sales teams.

What to learn: `XLOOKUP` or `INDEX` with `MATCH`, `SUMIFS` and `COUNTIFS`, `IF` and `IFS`, text and date functions, pivot tables, conditional formatting, data validation and Power Query for repeatable cleaning.

First-job level: you can take a raw export of a few thousand rows, clean it, summarise it with a pivot table and answer a specific question in under an hour without searching for every formula.

How to prove it: share a workbook with a clean data sheet, a summary sheet and a short notes tab explaining your steps. Our guide to [Excel for data analysis](/articles/excel-for-data-analysts) covers the exact functions with worked examples.

### SQL

SQL is the skill most likely to decide whether you clear a technical round. Company data sits in databases and warehouses, and analysts are expected to pull it themselves rather than wait for an engineer.

What to learn: `SELECT`, `WHERE`, `GROUP BY`, `HAVING`, `INNER` and `LEFT` joins, `CASE`, CTEs and window functions such as `ROW_NUMBER`, `RANK` and `LAG`.

First-job level: given two or three related tables, you can answer questions like "revenue by city for delivered orders last quarter" or "each customer's first purchase" correctly, and explain why you chose a particular join.

How to prove it: publish a GitHub repository with a dataset, your queries, and a README listing each business question with its answer. Start with this [beginner's guide to SQL for data analysis](/articles/sql-for-data-analysis) if you are new to it.

### A BI tool: Power BI or Tableau

Dashboards are how most stakeholders consume analysis. Power BI is very common in Indian enterprises, while Tableau appears often in consulting and some product companies. Pick one and go deep.

What to learn: connecting to data, basic data modelling (fact and dimension tables, relationships), calculated measures (DAX in Power BI), filters and slicers, and a clean page layout.

First-job level: you can build a two or three page dashboard with KPIs at the top, trends over time, a breakdown by segment, and filters that a manager can use without help.

How to prove it: publish the dashboard (Power BI's publish-to-web or Tableau Public), add screenshots to your portfolio, and write two or three sentences on the decisions it supports.

### Statistics

You do not need a statistics degree, but you do need to avoid confident wrong answers. Many analyst mistakes come from comparing averages with very different sample sizes or reading a correlation as a cause.

What to learn: mean, median and mode, standard deviation and percentiles, distributions and outliers, correlation, sampling, confidence intervals, and the logic of an A/B test.

First-job level: you can explain why the median order value might be more honest than the mean, and say whether a difference between two groups is large enough to act on.

How to prove it: include one project with a clear before-and-after comparison or A/B style test, with your reasoning written in plain language. This overview of [statistics for data analysts](/articles/statistics-for-data-analysts) lists the concepts that come up most.

### Python with pandas

Python is no longer optional at many product companies and startups, though some entry-level roles still list it as "good to have". It helps most when data is too large or too messy for Excel, or when a task needs to be repeated every week.

What to learn: reading CSV and Excel files, `groupby`, `merge`, filtering, handling missing values with `fillna` and `dropna`, working with dates, and plotting with matplotlib or seaborn. Jupyter notebooks are the usual working environment.

First-job level: you can load a dataset, clean it, answer five or six questions with pandas and produce charts, all in a readable notebook.

How to prove it: a well-commented notebook on GitHub that a reviewer can run top to bottom without errors.

### Using AI tools responsibly

Generative AI tools such as ChatGPT, Copilot and Gemini are now part of many analysts' daily work: drafting SQL, explaining an unfamiliar formula, or suggesting chart options. Employers increasingly value people who use them well, which means checking every output against the data rather than trusting it. Treat AI as a fast junior assistant, not as the analyst.

## Non-technical data analyst skills

Tools get you shortlisted. These skills decide whether people trust your work once you are in the job.

### Business sense

An analyst who knows that a quick-commerce company lives or dies on order frequency and delivery cost will ask better questions than one who only knows syntax. Business sense means understanding how the company makes money and which metrics move that.

How to build it: for every project, write down who the decision-maker is and what they would do differently based on your answer. Read annual reports or investor presentations of listed Indian companies in a sector you like, and note the metrics they highlight.

How to prove it: in interviews, connect every finding to an action. "Fashion has the highest return rate" is a fact; "Fashion returns are concentrated in two size ranges, so the size chart is the first fix to test" is analysis.

### Framing the question

Stakeholders often ask vague questions such as "Why are sales down?" A good analyst clarifies before querying: down compared with what period, in which region, measured as revenue or as orders?

How to prove it: start each portfolio project with a one-paragraph problem statement and the specific questions you set out to answer.

### Communication

You will spend a surprising amount of time explaining numbers to people in sales, operations and finance. That means short emails, clear slides and the ability to answer "so what?" in one sentence. In many Indian teams this also means being comfortable explaining in both Hindi and English, depending on the audience.

How to prove it: write a short summary at the top of every project, in plain English, that a manager could read in thirty seconds.

### Data storytelling

Storytelling is communication with structure: context, the finding, the evidence, and the recommendation. It also means choosing the right chart, removing clutter, and putting the key number in the title rather than making readers hunt for it.

How to prove it: turn one project into a five-slide deck or a one-page report, and practise presenting it in five minutes.

### Attention to detail

One wrong join can double-count revenue and quietly undermine everything else you report. Good analysts sanity-check totals against a known figure, look at row counts after every join, and question numbers that look too good.

How to prove it: document your checks in your project notes, for example "row count before join: 10,000; after join: 10,000; no duplicates introduced".

## What level is enough for a first job?

Freshers often delay applying because they feel they have not "finished" learning. A practical bar for applying to entry-level analyst roles:

- You can clean and summarise data in Excel with pivot tables and lookups without looking up basics.
- You can write SQL with joins, grouping and a simple window function, and explain your logic.
- You have built at least one interactive dashboard in Power BI or Tableau.
- You can explain mean versus median, outliers and correlation versus causation.
- You have basic pandas, or a clear plan to learn it, depending on the roles you target.
- You have three to four portfolio projects, each with a clear question, method and finding.

You do not need machine learning, cloud certifications or five programming languages for a first analyst role. Those belong to data science or data engineering tracks. Salaries for fresher analysts vary widely by city, company type and skill set; check current ranges on AmbitionBox, Glassdoor and Naukri for the specific roles and cities you are targeting rather than relying on a single headline number.

## How to prove your skills to employers

Recruiters cannot see your skills, only evidence of them. A simple proof map:

- Excel: a workbook with a cleaning log, pivot summaries and a clear answer.
- SQL: a GitHub repo of queries, each tied to a written business question.
- Power BI or Tableau: a published dashboard link plus screenshots.
- Statistics: one project with a comparison or test and a plain-language conclusion.
- Python: a clean, runnable Jupyter notebook.
- Communication and storytelling: a short deck or one-page summary for at least one project.
- Business sense: a recommendation section in every project.

Projects that combine several skills work best, for example SQL to extract, Python or Excel to clean, Power BI to present and a written summary to recommend. These [data analyst portfolio project ideas](/articles/data-analyst-portfolio-project-ideas) are scoped for exactly that. Put the links at the top of your resume and on your LinkedIn profile, not buried at the end.

## A sensible order to learn data analyst skills

If you are starting from zero, this sequence builds each skill on the last:

1. Excel, because it teaches you to think in rows, columns and summaries.
2. SQL, because it is the most tested skill and builds on the same ideas.
3. Statistics basics, learned alongside real datasets rather than in isolation.
4. Power BI or Tableau, to present what you have found.
5. Python with pandas, once you are comfortable with data concepts.
6. Storytelling and business sense, practised in every project from the start.

How long this takes depends on your starting point and how much you practise on real data, so measure progress by finished projects rather than hours watched.

## Frequently asked questions

### What skills are required for a data analyst fresher?

A fresher should have working Excel, SQL with joins and aggregation, one BI tool such as Power BI, and basic statistics. Python with pandas is increasingly expected at startups and product companies. Communication and a portfolio of three to four projects matter as much as the tools.

### Is coding required to become a data analyst?

SQL is required for almost every analyst role, and it is a form of coding, though a gentle one. Python is required at some companies and optional at others. You can start in Excel and Power BI, but plan to learn SQL early.

### Which is more important for a data analyst, SQL or Python?

For most entry-level roles in India, SQL is more important because it is used daily and tested in nearly every interview. Python becomes more valuable as you move to larger datasets, automation or more advanced analysis. Learn SQL first, then add Python.

### Can I become a data analyst with only Excel?

Some MIS and reporting roles are mostly Excel-based, but most job descriptions titled "data analyst" also ask for SQL and a BI tool. Excel alone limits your options. Adding SQL and Power BI widens the set of roles you can apply for considerably.

### How do I show data analyst skills without work experience?

Build portfolio projects on real public datasets, publish them on GitHub and Tableau Public or Power BI, and write a clear summary for each. Mention specific outcomes in your resume, such as the question you answered and the tool you used. A well-documented project is the closest substitute for experience.
